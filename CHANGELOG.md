## 2026-08-30 — Global Map: country labels redrawn

The label layer looked wrong, and it turned out to be four separate faults stacked on one another rather than a styling preference.

- **Every country name was drawn half its own width off-centre (the big one).** `.map-country-label` carried `transform: translate(-50%,-50%)` to centre the text on its centroid. It never once applied: Leaflet positions a marker through an *inline* `transform`, which beats a stylesheet rule, and the divIcon is a 0×0 box, so a percentage translate resolves to zero anyway. Names were anchored at their left edge and ran right and down from the point they were drawn for — ETHIOPIA sat in Somalia, SUDAN on the South Sudan border, RUSSIA well east of the spot it marks, UNITED STATES 55px adrift. Centring now happens on an inner `<span>`, which Leaflet does not touch; measured offset across every label is 0px on both axes.
- **The halo was a drop shadow, not a casing.** Four *offset* shadows at 2px blur, and none on the diagonals, so each word carried a lopsided grey smear off its bottom-right that read as grime against the near-black ocean. Replaced with four zero-offset blurs stacked tight to loose: an even casing that hugs the glyph, dense enough at 1px to hold a hard letterform over a bright choropleth fill and soft enough by 6px to lift the word off a busy one. A hard 1px ring was tried and rejected — at this size it thickens every stem and the labels read a weight heavier than they are.
- **Weight and size dialled back, and a size hierarchy added.** 10px/600 near-white shouted over the risk colour, which is the actual reading. Now 9.5px/500 in a cooler off-white, with three tiers (11.5 / 9.5 / 8px, colour tracking the size) picked per label from the room its country has on screen. One flat size for Russia and for Yemen is most of what made the layer read as stamped on rather than drawn; the tier is re-picked on every zoom, so a country's name grows as you zoom into it.
- **Placement now measures instead of guessing.** The fit test compared `name.length * 6.2` against the country's widest ring — blind to letter-spacing, blind to the difference between "ILL" and "WMM", and blind to the fact that a country's widest point is usually nowhere near its label. Widths are now measured in the same DOM the labels are drawn in (re-measured on `document.fonts.ready`, since a width taken before Geist arrives is the fallback font's), and the room a label has is scan-converted from the polygon **at the label's own latitude** — Ethiopia is 14.8° at its widest and 9.5° where its name sits; Chile is 8.7° and about 1.5°. The bar is coverage rather than containment (the country must underlie three quarters of the word, half at the small tier), because at world zoom MEXICO is simply wider than Mexico and every real map either drops the name or lets it overhang a little. Net effect: 47 labels at world zoom against 36 before, none overlapping, and the shapes that genuinely cannot carry a horizontal name (Chile, Italy, Luxembourg) wait for a zoom that lets them.
- **Off-screen labels are culled.** The pass placed every qualifying label at every zoom — 100+ markers at zoom 4, most of them off screen, some of them winning collisions against names that were actually visible. It now considers only the padded viewport.
- **Phantom label removed.** The new measuring element carries `.map-country-label` so its metrics are the real ones, which also meant every `.map-country-label` query returned the ruler holding whatever it measured last. It now carries a `.map-label-ruler` marker class as well.
- Browser-verified (headless Chromium, local build): 47/47 labels centred to 0px; three tiers in use (9/16/22); zero label-on-label overlap; no stray labels outside the viewport at zoom 5; GERMANY promotes to the large tier on zoom-in; ruler hidden and excluded. Map suite still green — nav controls, pan clamp, legend filter, scenario paint restore, Egypt trade arcs (18), disturbance markers (98/102). Zero console errors.

## 2026-08-30 — Global Map: navigation controls, antimeridian repair, legend as filter

- **The map had no on-screen navigation at all.** Leaflet's `zoomControl` is off and `.leaflet-control` is `display:none`, so zoom was scroll-wheel only and there was no way back to the world view short of reloading the page. `globeZoom()` and `globeRecenter()` had been written for exactly this and nothing in the document ever called them. Added `#map-nav` — zoom in / zoom out / reset to world — anchored bottom-right to mirror the legend's bottom-left, in the dashboard's own glass chrome rather than Leaflet's. The zoom buttons disable at `minZoom`/`maxZoom` instead of silently no-opping, and the cluster steps left of the country side panel (`#tab-global.sp-open`) instead of hiding underneath it. `globeRecenter()` now also closes an open country panel: deselecting while the brief stayed open left the panel describing a country the map no longer highlighted.
- **Antimeridian smear FIXED (visual correctness).** Natural Earth clips at ±180, so Fiji, Russia (Chukotka) and Antarctica each arrive as a ring whose longitudes jump 179.9 → −179.9. Leaflet projects each vertex independently and joins them with a straight screen line, so that hop was drawn as a full-width horizontal band of the country's own risk colour laid over every country beneath it — a gold stripe across South America and the Pacific at Fiji's latitude, a thick green one across the Arctic at Chukotka's, on every load. New `unwrapAntimeridian()` walks each ring at load and carries a ±360° offset forward whenever consecutive longitudes differ by more than 180°, so the ring stays continuous (Chukotka runs on to 190°E rather than teleporting to 170°W); a ring that unwraps clean off the far side is re-seated by one world width. Non-crossing rings are untouched — all but three of them.
- **Fiji stops stealing a world-zoom label.** `widestRingDeg()` was written to stop Fiji outranking the United States for a label, but it measures min/max longitude inside a ring, which for a crossing ring is still the full 360° — so Fiji went on winning, and was labelled at world zoom while France and Germany were not. Unwrapping makes the measurement its real 1.6°.
- **Panning is fenced to the world.** The map draws exactly one world copy, so panning past its edges only ever revealed empty container background — and, with no controls, no way back. Added `maxBounds` (padded, `maxBoundsViscosity: 0.85`, so a flick still feels elastic) and `noWrap` + `bounds` on the Esri place-label raster, which until now tiled on forever past ±180 and floated a second Europe's worth of city names over blank background.
- **Tooltip no longer leaves the viewport.** The country hover card is `position:fixed` and was pinned at cursor +14/+14 regardless of where the cursor was, so hovering a country near the right edge or along the bottom pushed the score off-screen. It now flips to the other side of the cursor when it would overflow.
- **The risk legend is now the map's risk filter.** The always-on 5-band ramp names exactly the tiers the Filters dropdown offers, so each band is a real `<button>` that sets `activeFilters.risk` through the same `<select>` the dropdown drives — the two can never disagree, `clearFilters()` is untouched, and the filter count badge stays honest. Clicking the pressed band clears it; unselected bands dim so the legend reports the map's state; a Clear affordance appears only while a filter is live. Delegated from `#map-legend` rather than bound per node, because scenario paint mode swaps that element's innerHTML wholesale and restores it from a snapshot — the painted legend carries no `[data-risk]`, so clicks there fall through.
- Browser-verified (headless Chromium against a local build): no ring outside the poles spans >180° after unwrap; Fiji 1.61°; all 162 GeoJSON-matched centroids within tolerance of their capital coordinates, none out of range; zoom buttons drive the map and disable at the limits; pan clamps into the world; legend↔dropdown sync both directions; nav cluster clears the country panel; scenario paint swaps and restores the legend byte-identically and the delegated filter survives it; trade-flow arcs (Egypt, 18) and disturbance markers (98/102) still place. Zero console errors.

## 2026-07-18 — v45: Comtrade double-count correction + two dead panels revived

- **UN Comtrade USD values were overstated 3–11× for a subset of importers (CRITICAL, data correctness).** The public-preview endpoint returns the same trade several times over — once as an aggregate row, again split by transport mode (`motCode`), and again by second partner (`partner2Code`) — and `refresh_comtrade.py` summed every returned row. Verified live: GBR wheat 2024 read 3,548,448,325 USD against a true 887,112,081 (exactly 4.00×); DEU 3.05×; ESP ~11×; while EGY, ITA and NLD were already correct. Because the multiple is per-(importer, commodity) rather than per-importer, the file **mixed correct and inflated rows** and no single scale factor could repair it — cross-importer rankings and bar charts on the Commodity tab were unreliable. Fixed by `_clean_rows()` (keeps `motCode = 0 ∧ partner2Code = 0`); dataset rebuilt; `value_basis` and `_meta.notes` now name the filter so the provenance is machine-readable.
- **The stress-test engine was NOT affected.** All nine shock functions read the kilotonne-based curated flow atlas (`commodity_flows.json`) via `realShares`, not Comtrade USD. `scenario_spec.json`'s `sourced_flows` basis tier refers to that atlas. Scenario outputs are unchanged. The one indirect exposure is `ban`, which reads `supPct` — see the `_supplier_basis` note below.
- **New validator that would have caught it.** `validate_comtrade_unit_prices()` divides each Comtrade USD total by USDA PSD import tonnage and asserts the implied unit price sits inside a per-commodity band (2024 CIF reference, ~2× headroom). Two independent on-disk sources, no network. On the pre-fix file it fired on 19 importer/commodity pairs (TUR/soybeans 3,105 USD/t, ESP/soybeans 5,951, IDN/rice 10,841). It separately surfaced 5 *under*-band pairs (YEM/maize 19 USD/t, NGA/rice 47) — a distinct coverage or vintage gap, reported as such rather than conflated with duplication. Previously `comtrade_staples.json` was checked only for shape (`dict_or_empty`), which a file with every value 11× too large passes.
- **Disturbances food-price panel revived.** It read `LIVE.fao_ffpi`, a key never assigned (`LIVE.ffpi` is set at load), so the panel rendered its "not loaded" fallback in production. Month-on-month deltas additionally read a `previous` key the pipeline has never emitted; they now derive from `series[len-2]`, so the panel shows real movement (June 2026: cereals −4.0, oils +7.0, dairy −1.8, meat +0.5, sugar −5.4).
- **Daily-summary FFPI bullet no longer drops its index level.** `build_daily_summary.py` read `latest.value`; the field is `latest.fpi`.
- **Methodology cites the correction.** The Trade Flow formulas card carries a scoped v45 note distinguishing the (unaffected) kt atlas from the corrected USD values; the supplier-shares caption no longer claims "full Comtrade matrix".
- **Known follow-ups.** `_supplier_basis` (`build_fields.py`) picks each country's dominant staple by comparing inflated totals across commodities, so some picks may flip after the rebuild — `ALB`→coffee and `AUT`→coffee are the flags to check. Supplier shares are only self-normalising where the duplication was uniform per partner (GBR's 4.00× is; DEU's non-integer 3.05× is not), so a per-partner uniformity check is still outstanding. The in-app changelog is stale from v23.6 and does not list v41–v44.
